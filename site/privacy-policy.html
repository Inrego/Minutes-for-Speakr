Minutes for Speakr — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 September 2026

Minutes for Speakr (“the app”) is a client application for a self-hosted Speakr transcription server. The app is published by Rene Scott Simonsen (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what the app does and does not do with your data.

The short version

  • The app has no backend operated by us. It talks only to the Speakr server you configure.
  • We do not collect, receive, store, or have access to any of your data.
  • The app contains no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party tracking SDKs.
  • The app can record other apps' audio, not just your microphone, and it can delete recordings from your device after uploading them. Both are features you turn on, and both are described plainly below.

What the app handles, and where it goes

When you use the app, the following data is sent directly from your device to the Speakr server URL you enter — and nowhere else:

  • Audio recordings: audio you record in the app (from your microphone, from other apps playing on the device, or both), audio files you pick for upload, and audio files the auto-upload feature finds in folders you chose.
  • Recording metadata you provide: tags, notes, meeting date, language, and minimum/maximum speaker counts. For auto-uploaded files the app also sends the file name and the file's last-modified time, which the server uses as the meeting date.
  • Speaker names you assign during speaker review.
  • Transcripts and summaries are generated by your server and displayed in the app; they are not sent anywhere else.

We never see this data. It is processed by the server you control, under that server's own terms.

Recording other apps' audio

Besides the microphone, the app can capture audio that other apps play on your device. That can include the other side of a phone or video call, a meeting in a conferencing app, media playback, or anything else the device is playing at the time. If you record a call this way, the other participants' voices are in the recording.

  • You start it. System audio is a source you switch on for a recording session; it is off unless you turn it on.
  • Android captures other apps' audio through Android's media projection API. Every time you start a recording with system audio, Android shows its own screen-capture consent dialog and recording only proceeds if you accept. The permission is released when the recording stops and is never cached or reused for a later session. Only audio is captured — the app never reads, stores, or transmits screen pixels. Android only hands this API media, game, and unclassified audio; apps can opt their audio out, and ordinary phone-call audio is normally not available to it, so whether the other side of a call is captured depends on the calling app.
  • Windows captures system audio through the Windows audio loopback API, either everything the device is playing or only the audio of one app you chose.
  • The captured audio is written to a temporary file on the device and goes to one destination: the Speakr server you configured. It is not sent to us or to anyone else.

Auto-record on Windows

On Windows you can turn on auto-record: you choose which applications should trigger it (for example a meeting app), and when one of those applications starts using your microphone, the app starts a recording automatically with the sources you configured for that application. When the application releases the microphone and the speakers go quiet, the app asks you whether to stop and upload the recording or keep going. Auto-record is off by default and records nothing until you add at least one application to its list.

Auto-upload, folder access, and deletion of your files

The auto-upload feature watches folders you choose on your device, for example the output folder of a call-recorder or voice-recorder app. The app periodically scans those folders, uploads each new audio file to your server, and then — by design — deletes the original file from your device once the upload has succeeded. These are files written by other apps, not by this app. Delete-after-upload is what keeps a device that records calls all day from filling up.

  • If you set a minimum duration for a watched folder, audio files shorter than that are deleted without being uploaded. This is meant for the short accidental recordings (missed calls, pocket taps) a call recorder produces. It is off unless you set a value.
  • If a file cannot be deleted after upload, it stays on the device and the app shows an error for it in Auto-upload settings. Files are never deleted before the server has confirmed the upload.
  • On Android you pick each watched folder in Android's own folder picker (the Storage Access Framework). Android then grants the app access to that folder and its subfolders only, and the app keeps that grant so it can scan, read, and delete files there in the background without asking you again for each file. The grant does not extend to any other part of your storage: the app cannot see, browse, index, or upload anything outside the folders you picked, and you can take the access away at any time by removing the folder in Auto-upload settings or by uninstalling the app. The app does not use Android's “All files access” (MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE) permission. Earlier builds of the Android app did request All files access for this feature; that permission is being removed, and if a build you have still asks for it, it is used only for the watched-folder scan and delete described here and for nothing else.
  • On Windows the same watch, upload, and delete behaviour applies to the folders you pick; no special permission is involved.

Microphone-usage monitoring on Windows

To know when to start an auto-recording, the Windows app reads a Windows registry area (CapabilityAccessManager\ConsentStore\microphone under the current user) about every two seconds. Windows records there which applications are currently using the microphone. The app uses this only to decide whether one of the applications you listed has started or stopped using the microphone.

  • This information is read locally and is never transmitted anywhere.
  • To let you pick applications from a list instead of typing their names, the app keeps a short local list (at most 30 entries) of application names it has recently seen using the microphone, with the time each was last seen. That list lives in the app's settings on your PC and is never sent anywhere. No audio and no other details about those applications are recorded.
  • For the stop prompt the app also reads the peak output level of your speakers locally, to notice when a meeting has gone quiet. Only the level is read, not the audio, and it is not stored or transmitted.
  • This monitoring exists on Windows only. The Android and iOS apps do nothing of the kind.

Credentials and local storage

  • Your server URL and API token are stored in your device's operating system secure storage: Android Keystore on Android, the Keychain on iOS, and on Windows a file in the app's data folder encrypted with the Windows Data Protection API (DPAPI), readable only by your Windows user account. They are not transmitted to us or any third party, and are not written to logs.
  • Audio you record in the app is written to a temporary directory and deleted after it is uploaded to your server. If the upload fails, the file stays until the upload succeeds or you discard the recording.
  • Your app settings (watched folders and their options, auto-record application list, recently seen applications, default tags and speaker counts) are stored locally in the app's data and never leave the device.

Permissions and why they are used

  • Microphone — to record audio for live recording.
  • Capture other apps' audio (Android media projection) — to record system audio alongside the microphone, as described above. Requested per session through Android's own consent dialog.
  • Access to folders you pick (Android) — a per-folder grant from Android's folder picker, used to scan the folders you watch for auto-upload and to delete recordings from them after they have been uploaded, as described above. It covers only the folders you chose. The app does not request All files access.
  • Phone state (Android) — to trigger an auto-upload scan after a phone call ends, so call recordings can be picked up without opening the app. Call contents, phone numbers, and call-log details are not read or transmitted.
  • Foreground service / notifications — to keep recording and uploads running reliably while the app is in the background, and to show you that a recording is in progress.
  • Run after restart — on Android, so scheduled auto-upload scans resume after the device reboots; on Windows, an optional start-with-Windows setting you can turn on in the app.

Analytics, advertising, and third parties

The app integrates no analytics, crash-reporting, advertising, or tracking SDKs. No usage data is collected. The app's typefaces are bundled with the app, so it contacts no font CDN or any other third-party host — the only network destination the app ever contacts is the Speakr server you point it at.

Data retention and deletion

Because we hold no data, there is nothing for us to retain or delete. To delete your recordings, transcripts, or account data, manage them on your own Speakr server, or clear the app's stored credentials from within the app (Settings) or by uninstalling. Files the app deletes from your device after upload (see above) are gone from the device; the uploaded copy is on your server.

Children

Minutes for Speakr is a productivity tool for managing your own transcription server and is not directed at children under 13.
